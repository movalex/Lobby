# A14: render performance of the heavy board

**Status: PARTIAL.** The workstation half ran 3 of 3 times (both engines, same method, same browser).
**The laptop half is UNRUN**: there is no laptop here. Plan §8.2 A14 ("reported", gate in §8.5).

## Method

The board is e2e/perf's heavy board (`e2e/perf/seed.ts`): 20 frames, 4,500 sticky notes each with a label,
200 images.

- **Excalidraw** (`e2e/spike/tests/render-a14-heavy.spec.ts`, page `/spike/render-heavy`): the same layout
  and pseudo-random texts and colours, built in the page with `convertToExcalidrawElements`: 20 frames +
  4,500 `stickynote` + 4,500 bound labels (Nunito) + 200 `image` elements = **9,220 elements** (6.1 MB of
  JSON), notes with `created: null`, images with `status: 'saved'`. The scene is stored on the fixture
  server and opened like a board: fetch the JSON, `updateScene(..., NEVER)`, `setViewport(fit: contain)`,
  two frames painted, `document.fonts.ready`; then Lobby's loader fetches the visible images first (small
  generated PNGs, 320 × 200, same-origin through the preview proxy, at most 6 at a time).
- **tldraw baseline** (`render-a14-tldraw.spec.ts`): e2e/perf unchanged: the real api + sync + preview
  worker (`e2e/perf/stack.ts`, E2E_PORT_BASE 7820), `seedHeavyBoard` (200 uploaded JPEGs with previews,
  4,720 shapes, tags, 40 comment threads) and `openBoard`.
- **Both** in the same browser: Playwright's headless Chromium shell with e2e/perf's GPU flags
  (`--enable-gpu --ignore-gpu-blocklist --use-angle=d3d11`, `e2e/perf/lib.ts`), 1536 × 864 at DPR 1.25,
  the same pan (`e2e/spike/fixtures/perf-kit.ts`: e2e/perf/fps.ts's method: the pointer rests over the
  canvas, a scripted camera move every animation frame for 3 s, a 600 CSS px sine sideways at up to
  1,200 px/s and half of it vertically, 3 pans per view; fps = rAF callbacks per second; plus one traced pan
  for the frames the compositor drew and the main thread's busy share). Views: fit, 0.25 (centred on frame
  0) and 1 (frame 0's corner). Open time = wall clock from `page.goto` to the painted board, cold (a new
  context, empty HTTP cache), 3 times per run.
- **GPU or software:** GPU. WebGL renderer `ANGLE (NVIDIA, NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090 Direct3D11 vs_5_0
  ps_5_0, D3D11)`, Chromium's `2d_canvas` feature status `enabled` (read through CDP `SystemInfo`), headless
  shell 153.0.8010.12. Machine: the Threadripper 9970X / RTX 5090 workstation of the review.

```sh

SPIKE_A14_TLDRAW=1 SPIKE_A14_RUN=<n> E2E_PORT_BASE=7820 SPIKE_WEB_PORT=7720 pnpm --filter @draftroom/e2e spike render-a14-tldraw
```

Reports: `e2e/test-results/spike-7720-a14/a14-run-<n>.json`, `a14-tldraw-run-<n>.json`.

## Findings

1. **Excalidraw cannot fit the heavy board.** Its own fit (`setViewport({ fit: 'contain' })` →
   `zoomToFitBounds`, `@excalidraw/excalidraw/dist/dev/chunk-FN755BR4.js:501`) and every user zoom go
   through `getNormalizedZoom`, which clamps to [0.1, 30] (`chunk-FN755BR4.js:642-644`, `MIN_ZOOM = 0.1`,
   `@excalidraw/common/dist/dev/index.js:1898`). The heavy board needs 0.054 in a 1536 × 864 view, so
   "fit" stops at 0.1 with 3,488 of 9,220 elements in view (38%). `updateScene({ appState: { zoom } })`
   does accept 0.054 (the "true fit" row), but the wheel cannot zoom out past 0.1. tldraw's fit shows the
   whole board (zoom 0.05). Lobby's "zoom to fit" / overview needs either the programmatic path (and a
   decision about what the wheel does below 0.1) or the level-of-detail overview of §8.5.
2. **Fit fps (same method, same browser): both engines are below the §8.5 gate of 24 fps.** The whole
   board: Excalidraw (true fit, 0.054) 13.3 / 14.6 / 14.5 fps (run medians; median 14.5), tldraw (fit, 0.05)
   17.6 / 16.4 / 18.8 (median 17.6): for the same picture Excalidraw is ~18% slower. At Excalidraw's own
   clamped fit (0.1, 38% of the board in view) 21.5 / 19.1 / 19.6. The §8.5 conditional ("below 24 fps
   at fit": the level-of-detail pass, `docs/EXCALIDRAW-MIGRATION.md:1970`) applies.
3. **0.25 and 1: both engines above the 30 fps gate, comparable.** At 0.25 Excalidraw 57 / 47.7 / 50.4
   (single pans 42.8-59.7), tldraw 55 / 53.4 / 54; the traced pans agree (55.8-57.6 against 52.8-56.4
   frames/s). At 1: 60 against 59.3. The earlier Excalidraw series gave 57.4-58.3 at 0.25: the spread
   between series is larger than the gap between the engines.
4. **Render-bound.** At fit the main thread is ~100% busy during the pan (trace), and the frames drawn
   match the rAF rate: the cost is Excalidraw's static-canvas render of every visible element per frame.
   The first pan at each zoom-out view is usually the slowest (10.9-15.8 fps: caches being built).
5. **Drag previews are cheap.** A 100-note drag preview through `RenderOverrides` (offsets replaced
   every frame, camera still, zoom 1) ran at 60 fps with 179-181 override snapshots in 3 s: about 60
   override updates per second, the §8.4 number.
6. **Images.** Lobby's loader fetched all 200 images, never more than 6 at a time, 0 failures. Handing
   them to the engine one `addFiles` at a time kept the board busy for 4.9-6.5 s after the first paint
   (each call repaints the scene, `index.js:43789-43796`); with the batching now in the loader:
   every visible image is drawn 0.76-1.83 s after the first paint (median 1.28 s), with 4-8 `addFiles`
   calls for the 200 images.
7. **Open.** Excalidraw: 0.67-0.76 s from `page.goto` to the painted board; of that, the engine is
   ready at ~0.47 s (JS loaded and mounted), and fetching (6.1 MB over loopback), parsing, `updateScene`
   of 9,220 elements, `setViewport` and the first paint take ~0.1 s together (fetch 26-31 ms, parse 7 ms,
   `updateScene` 25 ms, first paint ~35 ms). This is **not comparable** with tldraw's open here
   (3.7-4.0 s), which includes the real api, the ticket, the sync socket and the store hydration: the
   Excalidraw page has no sync server yet (spike B measures welcome bytes and time). The §8.5 open gate (≤ 3.4 s) cannot be judged
   from this number; it says the engine side is not the bottleneck.
8. **The review's numbers do not reproduce in this browser.** tldraw here (headless shell, GPU): open
   3.7-4.0 s, fit 16.4-18.8, 0.25 53.4-55, 1 59.3 fps, against the review's branded Chrome 3.36 s and
   26-32 / 49-54 / 58-60 (and §8.5's "today's median is 29" at fit). e2e/perf's own budget notes
   that the 600 px sweep at fit measured 16-21 fps and the 26 fps figure is its gentle 60 px pan (`e2e/perf/budget.json`). The comparison that counts is the
   same-method one above.
9. **Echo: 0.** Opening, fitting, panning, 200 image loads and the drag previews changed no element
   version (EchoProbe: 0 ops, 0 drift).
10. Heap after the runs: 46-71 MB used (V8 heap; 41-167 MB in the earlier series; varies with GC timing).

## Pass criterion and status

§8.5's Go row asks for the heavy board to pan at ≥ 30 fps at 0.25 and 1, open in ≤ 3.4 s and pan at
≥ 24 fps at fit (`docs/EXCALIDRAW-MIGRATION.md:1969`), on the workstation and the laptop.
On this workstation neither engine reaches 24 fps at fit with this method, Excalidraw's own fit does not
show the whole board, and the open time is not comparable (no sync server in the Excalidraw page). The
check is **reported** (§8.2) and the numbers are above; the **laptop half is UNRUN**. Hence PARTIAL.

## Deviations from the plan, and why

- The Excalidraw scene is generated in the page with the same layout and texts as e2e/perf's seed, not
  migrated from a tldraw board (the converter and the staging-board conversions are spike B's), and it opens from a static JSON file instead of
  a sync welcome (spike B's). The tldraw baseline uses e2e/perf's real stack unchanged.
- The images are small generated PNGs (320 × 200) instead of the uploaded JPEGs of e2e/perf's seed (which
  go through the api's upload and preview worker, not available to a page without the stack).
- Both engines were measured in Playwright's headless shell with GPU flags, not the branded Chrome of the
  review; the review's numbers are therefore not the baseline (finding 8).
- No laptop: the second half of A14 is UNRUN.

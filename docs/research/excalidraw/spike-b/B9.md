### What changed (`apps/sync/src/room`: new modules and small edits to `element-room.ts`; the client's transport)

1. **Snapshot reuse** (`SNAPSHOT_REUSE`): the cached gzip snapshot serves every joiner while the board is
   at most 500 rows and 60 s past its clock, or unchanged; each joiner gets the rows since the snapshot's
   clock as a `changes` catch-up right after it, as the 5 s cache already did. A delete drops the cache at
   once (W5-02). At L1's 40 rows/s the 3 MB snapshot is built every 12 s instead of every 5 s; at L2's 20
   edits/s the 8 MB one once in the 15 s ramp instead of 3 times. Plan §2.4.5 and §2.13 say so now.
2. **Catch-ups from the stored strings** (`catch-up.ts`): a catch-up op is the stored `data` string wrapped
   once (a tombstone is parsed and redacted, W5-02); the welcome's other fields are stringified once and
   the ops JSON spliced in. The ops of the last 8 `since` values are cached per clock (cleared by every
   commit), so a reconnect storm asking for the same rows gets the same bytes. `store.sinceRaw`.
3. **Roster batching** (`roster-batch.ts`): joins and leaves of one macrotask go out as one `roster`
   frame per recipient, in order, flushed before every presence tick; a joiner never gets its own join.
4. **Commit pacing** (`pacing.ts`, `DRAIN_PACING`): a busy room (4 or more commits in the last second)
   holds the first push of a batch for 40 ms so the pushes of the next few tens of milliseconds share one
   commit, one fsync and one fan-out; a quiet room never waits. This is §2.13's step 1 by its intent
   ("adds ≤ 50 ms to acks"); B9's tune1 run showed the floor alone did not reduce commits. A busy board's
   acks move one bucket (server p95 ≤ 50 → ≤ 100 ms, target 150; mean 19 → 32 ms).
5. **Presence frames**: each changed peer is serialized once per tick and the per-recipient frames are
   joined from those strings (the same JSON as before, `presence-frames.test.ts`).
6. **Room open**: the store's statements are prepared on first use, and the cache rows (group ids, the
   bound-label flag, the guest creator) are read in SQL with `json_extract`, so no row is parsed in JS at
   open (`store.ts liveIndexRows`).
7. **Large frames in slices** (`outbound.ts`, `DEFER_ABOVE_BYTES` 8 KB, `FLUSH_SLICE_MS` 5): a welcome
   with its roster or catch-up, the gzip snapshot and a frame drag's `changes` are written by a
   process-wide flusher in 5 ms slices on `setImmediate`, not inside the task that queued them; small
   frames (acks, cursors, ordinary `changes`) are still written at once. Per-session order is unchanged
   (a small frame queued behind a deferred one waits; a writer's `changes` still precede its ack).
8. **Boot**: `server.ts` turns on Node's compile cache (`module.enableCompileCache()`, V8 code cache
   under `os.tmpdir()`), then loads `server-main.ts`; the restarted process skips the compilation
   (`DRAFTROOM_COMPILE_CACHE=off` turns it off). Locally the boot went from about 370 to 290 ms of wall
   time.
9. **Client reconnects with jitter** (`socket.ts` `jitteredDelay`, ±30 % of each backoff step, longest
   wait 6.5 s): a restart no longer gets every tab back in the same instant. The thin load client does
   the same; the harness replaces a client that does not come back on its own only after 12 s
   (`HARNESS_RECONNECT_AFTER_MS`, above the longest backoff; it was 5 s, and r2 above replaced 9
   clients that were still coming back).

## Profiles

From `e2e/load/profiles.ts`, each sentence of §2.13 as a parameter.

**L1**: 260 sessions. The workshop board `l1-main` has 3,798 elements: 18 frames of 100 notes and 180
bound arrows, 36 of them elbow. Its 200 sessions:
- 29 gesture editors: one gesture every 3 s ± 30 %. Half are drags of 0.8 s, relayed at 20 Hz (ids once,
  then deltas) and committed at pointer-up. The other half are recolours or nudges.
- 1 editor drags a 100-note frame every 30 s.
- 10 typists: each holds the lease on its own note and pushes text every 500 ms, in 8 s bursts with 4 s
  pauses.
- 60 editors and 95 viewers who only watch. Their cursors move at 20 Hz for 5 s, then stay idle for 5 s.
- 5 measuring viewers. The first is the presence probe; the others read its cursor and parse every
  change for A→B visibility.

The rest of L1: 20 light boards (`l1-s00` … `l1-s19`, 85 elements each), 3 editors each, one push every
5 s. The join ramp puts every session on within 60 s. The NAS script kills sync at minute 8 of the
generator's clock.

Planned rate on `l1-main`: about 23 pushes/s. The budget says "~20/s on the big board" and "about 30
writes/s" counting the fits.

**L2**: the heavy board `l2-heavy` (9,220 elements), with 10 editors at 2 one-shot edits/s each (20
edits/s in total), 10 measuring viewers (one of them the probe) and 10 viewers.

**Thin receivers (new in this round, `thin.ts`).** A full engine core per session holds its own copy of
the board and rebuilds the scene array for every remote change: about 9 MB of live heap and about 380 KB
allocated per remote change per session on L1's board (O3's local measurement; a 260-session L1 with
full cores reached 7.7 GB and a client event-loop p99 of 650–700 ms on the workstation). The load
container has 4 CPUs and 2 GB, so most receive-only watchers run a thin session: the same socket,
ticket, `hello`, reconnect backoff and presence rate as O2's client, no scene, no core, never a push. The
server cannot tell them apart. **The meter measures at the socket** (bytes by type, closes, welcomes,
`changes` arrivals for A→B visibility, the probe's cursor for presence delay), so a thin measuring
session loses nothing. L1 keeps 51 full cores on `l1-main` (40 editors, 10 editor-role watchers for
B10's echo check, the presence probe) and 60 on the light boards; L2 keeps 12 (10 editors, the probe
and one more measuring viewer). The thin sessions all run on load worker 0, which has no engine core
to run, and the full cores round-robin on workers 1–4 (`runner.ts` `partitionPlans`); the NAS smoke run
before this change had one worker at a 677 ms event-loop p99 while the server answered pushes in ≤ 50 ms.

## What a report holds

Per run, `e2e/load/report.ts` writes `<profile>-<tag>.json` and `.md`. Each entry below names where its
number comes from:

- **Latency**:
  - ack: the push log against the acks;
  - A→B visibility through the server: an editor's acked edit `(id, v)` against its arrival in the
    measuring sessions' `changes` frames;
  - presence delay: the probe's cursor encodes its send time in a private region of the scene, which the
    40-cursor cap keeps for the measuring sessions, whose view is that region.
- **Protocol**: pushes/s, ops/s, rejects by code, partial denies, dropped fits (`fitDropped`), notices,
  closes by code, lease grants and denies.
- **Network**:
  - bytes/s to the clients, in total, per session and by message type;
  - per sampled session, the drag relay's id lists and deltas separately, and the cursor bytes;
  - welcome JSON and snapshot bytes.
- **Drag relay**: starts/s and ids per start.
- **Joins**, and the **restart**: sessions back, and all back in N ms after the kill. A client that does
  not reconnect by itself is replaced by the harness after 5 s; that count is reported.
- **Acked-but-missing**: each editor's own notes, read back through the test snapshot.
- **Echo (B10)**: how many watchers pushed.
- **Server**: sync's `/internal/metrics` scraped every 10 s, all names kept. The report maps the
  event-loop and RSS names once O1 names them.
- **Host**: the NAS sampler's `docker stats` every 10 s (CPU as % of the 0.5 CPU quota, memory MB) and
  `du -sm` every 30 s.
- **Targets**: the §2.13 targets, each marked met, missed or not measured.
- The clients' own event-loop p99 per thread, which shows whether a run was client-bound.

## Deviations from §8.3's listing (on purpose)

1. **Test hooks.** Sync also runs with `DRAFTROOM_TEST_HOOKS=1` (never with `NODE_ENV=production`). The
   load generator reads every board back through the test snapshot for acked-but-missing.
2. **Fresh boards per run.** The data folder, at exactly that path, is emptied and regenerated before
   each run, because a run changes its boards.
3. **Secrets file.** `run.env` is written by `write-run-env.ts` in a `--network none` container and
   removed after each run. No value from `.env.deploy.local` went to the NAS.
4. **The planted over-cap board is 40,000 notes of 60 characters** (`--overcap-notes 40000
   --overcap-chars 60`, 25–166 MB on disk), not the earlier default of 60,000 notes of 2,000 characters.
   Measured locally with O4's queue at the 98 MB runtime cap: a board of long texts hits the **60 s time
   budget** first (6,000 notes × 2,000 chars: `time-budget` after 60.0 s; 8,000 and 12,000 the same),
   which is a different failure than §8.3 asks for; a board of many short notes passes the cap within
   seconds (12,000 × 60: converts, peak 77 MB; 20,000 × 60: converts, peak 94.6 MB; **40,000 × 60:
   `out-of-memory` after 5.1 s**).
5. **Unconverted tldraw boards hold notes only** (no frames or images: the generator has no validated
   tldraw factories for them). `cul-heavy` has 4,500 notes, `cul-second` 600.
6. **Presence-mode comparison.** O1's room has no switch: a board with at most 41 sessions gets one frame
   serialized once for everyone, a larger board per-recipient frames with the 40-cursor cap. L1's
   200-session board is therefore always in the per-view mode; the comparison run forces the shared
   branch with the `presence-shared-hook.ts` preload (`CURSORS_PER_RECIPIENT` raised for that process
   only).
7. **Five load workers on four CPUs** (`--threads 5`, worker 0 the thin sessions), each worker with a
   360 MB old-generation cap so four full-core workers fit the 2 GB limit.
8. **The kill at minute 8 counts from the generator's clock** (its `t0` file), not from `docker run`
   (`nas-killer.sh`); the kill file gives the restart numbers from the kill command.

### Finding 1: a local edit is lost with nothing pushed (O2's engine core)

In diag1 the missed edit was never pushed: after A's `update(N, {x: 44})`, A's socket sent no push, A's
scene held the old `x` (27) again, the core had nothing dirty and showed Saved, the server held 27 at
version 9, and B, correctly, showed 27. The mechanism, as O2's core reads
(`apps/web/src/canvas/sync/core/engine.ts`):

- `onChanges` → `applyScene(affected)` builds the **whole** element array from `this.currentOrder`,
  which is the scene **as the core last scanned it** (`readScene` in `scan`), and installs it with
  `scene.apply` (`updateScene`, `CaptureUpdateAction.NEVER`);
- Excalidraw reports a scene change through `onChange` after React commits the update, not inside
  `updateScene`; so between a local `updateScene` (a facade write here; a user's drag end, recolour or
  keystroke equally) and the core's next `scan`, a WebSocket message task can run;
- if a `changes` frame lands in that window, `applyScene` overwrites the un-scanned local edit with the
  core's stale copy; the scan that follows sees the core's own apply, finds nothing changed, and
  nothing is ever pushed. The edit is gone without an error, a toast or a "Not saved".

Reproduced alone by **`e2e/spike-b/tests/b9-probe-lost-edit.spec.ts`** (opt-in `SPIKE_B_PROBES=1`; one
browser, a small board, 40 facade moves per phase, stack 7880): **control 0/40 lost; with a remote
writer moving another note at 50 Hz, 1/40 lost**, and the lost edit is exactly the one edit in which the
core's `applied` event (a remote apply) ran between the `update` and the core's next `onSceneChange`
(1.3 ms later): `localNow` 1,100, `localLater` 217 (the old value), `pushed: false`. Under L2 on the heavy
board, where the main thread is saturated and the window is longer, 1 to 8 of 30 edits per run were lost.

Why the other checks did not catch it: convergence (B11, the hash checks of every spec) compares every
map with the snapshot, and a lost edit leaves them all equal (the local scene is reset to the server's
copy). Only a check that looks for the edit itself (B9's watch, B1's "all three kept", L6's "A's text
intact") can see it, and only when a remote frame lands in the window.

A fix direction for O2 (not tried here): before any server apply (`onChanges`, `onAckMessage`,
`afterWelcome`), scan the live scene first (`this.scan(this.scene.read())`: `read()` returns
Excalidraw's current elements synchronously after `updateScene`), so un-scanned local edits become dirty
and `rebuild` keeps them over the server copy; or build the applied array from `scene.read()` instead of
`currentOrder`.

### Finding 2: the browsers' main threads are saturated, by CanvasPort.bounds in the overlay layers (O2, O5)

Per-edit stages in diag4 (no trace; ms after A's `update`, p50 at 100% zoom; `bMain` is when B's core
received the frame, from a listener on it; network-level times are within a few ms of the page's):

| A's core sends the push (`aCoreMs`) | 567 (787) | 254 (962) |
| B's core receives the `changes` frame (`bMainMs`) | 1,000 (1,399) | 262 (1,793) |
| B's port reports the value (`appliedMs`) | 1,233 (1,755) | 305 (2,115) |

- **A** takes 567 ms from its `update` to its push (the core coalesces for 50 ms): the scan that finds
  the edit waits behind other work on A's main thread.
- **Between A's push and B's receipt, 433 ms** at 100% (8 ms at fit, p50). The server is not it: in the
  same run it answered every push in ≤ 5 ms p50 and ≤ 50 ms p95 (`lobby_receive_to_ack_ms`, bucket

`bounds` is `CanvasPort.bounds(id)` (O2's `apps/web/src/canvas/port-impl.ts:184`): `byId(id)` is a
linear `find` over every element, and each call builds `new Map(all().map(...))` of all 9,220 elements
for `getCommonBounds`. The overlay layers call it on every `changed` and `viewportChanged` event
(`usePortTick(port, ['changed', 'viewportChanged'])`); the image card layer (O5's
`apps/web/src/canvas/spike/features/viewer.tsx`, `makeCardLayer`) calls it for every image on the board,
200 here, on screen or not. So each remote change or scroll step costs about 200 × (a 9,220-element
find + a 9,220-entry Map): about 170 ms. The caller is inferred (the only per-image loop, and the
images are the only difference between the two boards); the function and its cost are measured.

That is where B9's main thread goes: about 18 remote `changes` frames per second each trigger a
`changed` event and so a card-layer pass. A's scan and push queue behind it, B's receipt, apply and
paint queue, and finding 1's window widens. It is also why the perf-fps smoke run measured 4–7 fps
(PERF.md). Directions for O2 and O5 (not tried here): an id → element map for `bounds` and `get`
(Excalidraw keeps one: `getSceneElementsMapIncludingDeleted`), no per-call Map; the layers compute
bounds only for elements in the viewport and only when their own inputs change. Then re-measure; if the
apply itself is still too slow, coalesce server applies to one per animation frame.
